01THE STARTING POINT

The AI Opportunities
Action Plan.

Published in January 2025, the Action Plan set out the beginnings of a national strategy for the United Kingdom to take advantage of AI.

The government accepted all fifty of its recommendations: on compute infrastructure, data, skills, safety and regulation, adoption, and sovereign AI.

Project SAIL examines the implementation record and asks what government and business need to do next.

02THE IMPLEMENTATION RECORD

Progress is real.
Delivery is uneven.

The strategic framework is already producing dividends. But the review finds a consistent pattern: government moves fast where it controls the machinery directly, and far more slowly wherever delivery depends on other parties acting differently.

03THE CENTRAL DIAGNOSIS

The coordination
ceiling.

01 / BELOW THE CEILING

One capable team.

Where one capable team could build a thing and put it into service, delivery is strong.

Government is good at standing up a tool with a capable team behind it.

02 / ABOVE THE CEILING

Several parties
moving together.

Where a recommendation has required shared infrastructure, several departments moving together, or a change in the law, delivery thins into guidance, strategy documents and pilots.

The determining factor is the number of parties who have to act.

One capable team can build a thing and put it into service.

04ITERATING THE APPROACH

A project of
shared mobilisation.

Sprints suit problems that can be bounded. AI adoption also requires standing services, public consent and the ability to mend fragmentation.

THE SPRINT

Buying the vaccine.

A single clear objective. A ring-fenced budget. An empowered unit.

THE MOBILISATION

Getting it into arms.

General practices, pharmacies, volunteers and public trust earned neighbourhood by neighbourhood.

“The state can set the direction for all of these.
It cannot deliver any of them alone.”

06THE WAY FORWARD

Ten recommendations.
A role for each side.

A roadmap for the AI Taskforce and AI Minister to mobilise AI for the nation—with what business needs to put in alongside what government must do.

Proposals for the first twelve months. Some later milestones extend beyond that window; timings and accountable bodies below follow the review draft.

01AI economy + placesA Compute Accelerator for middle-scale computeHelp every mayoral and combined authority identify, match and unlock viable 10–100MW compute sites.

Extend the existing data-centre planning support function into a standing service: map sites and grid capacity, match developer partners, and support delivery through planning.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

Places identify sites and support development; data-centre businesses build the infrastructure for local growth.

DBIST

Operational by Budget 2026

02AI economy + placesCommunity Benefit Agreements for data centre hostsCreate a Community Benefit Agreement framework, backed by full business rates retention.

Publish a voluntary framework with benchmarked contributions and a menu of local recruitment, heat reuse, apprenticeships and facilities. Extend full business rates retention for a defined period to combined authorities hosting data centres.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

Places host infrastructure; developers publish community agreements and drop non-disclosure agreements in negotiations.

Accountable
AI Taskforce + MHCLG
Delivery

03AI economy + placesThe grid connection queue: build faster, decide who decidesBuild faster—and make clear who decides when connection capacity is scarce.

Give housing, heat and transport electrification stated priority on the distribution network. Where possible, route data centres to transmission, accelerate pre-studies and allocate capacity by readiness, flexibility and network need.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

Operators accept a separate route from homes and verified flexibility obligations in return for faster connections.

Accountable
DESNZ, with NESO

April 2027

04AI economy + peopleOne skills settlement for the AI economyBuild sector partnerships that connect skills standards to real workforce evidence.

Extend the financial-services compact model to construction, care, professional services and manufacturing, one sector at a time. Employers co-author AI skills standards and feed workforce data into the AI Economics Institute’s evidence base.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

The state opens its evidence and standards infrastructure; employers co-design standards and share workforce evidence.

Accountable
Skills England + AI Economics Institute + National Data Collaboration

First new sector within 12 months; three further sectors each year

05State + businessPublish a clear definition of Sovereign AIGive buyers a practical, risk-based way to assess sovereignty for each component and workload.

Publish a UK definition, then grade individual components and workloads rather than certify entire suppliers. Apply the classification as a scored procurement requirement for designated sensitive workloads, starting with health.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

The state provides clear sovereignty parameters; businesses deliver products against them.

Accountable
AI Taskforce + Government Commercial Agency
Delivery
Definition by April 2027; GCA schedule within 6 months, deployment within 12

06State + businessAggregate compute demand and grow compute accessCombine multi-year demand with tiered compute vouchers to support capacity and access.

Assemble committed demand from public bodies, researchers and voucher holders into a block that infrastructure builders can finance. Offer Innovate UK vouchers through Bridge AI, with hosting requirements proportionate to workload sensitivity.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

The state guarantees demand; businesses build capacity. Sensitive workloads remain under strict British legal and physical control.

Accountable
AI Taskforce; voucher delivery through Bridge AI
Delivery
Budget allocation in 2026; rollout by April 2027

07State + businessReport critical services’ dependence on AIRequire operators to report where AI dependence could threaten continuity, starting with a pathfinder such as health.

The Cabinet Office and NCSC should clarify existing coverage and establish dependency assessments: which functions depend on foreign-controlled systems, what happens if access fails, and what fallback exists. Evaluate the pathfinder before extending a statutory duty.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

Public services assess their dependence systematically; suppliers provide the information needed.

Accountable
Cabinet Office
Delivery

08State + businessGovernment as a demanding first customerUse purchasing power to accelerate both adoption at scale and innovation at the frontier.

GDS should lead adoption procurement from the centre of government. For capability procurement, the AI Taskforce should keep five designated innovation cases in play, with Sovereign AI Unit delivery and contracts focused on outcomes.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

The state provides ambitious early demand; businesses innovate to meet it.

Create partnerships for operational public-service data and a UKRI commissioning model for frontier datasets. Publish an interim dataset release list, with timelines and complexity assessments, and release commissioned frontier datasets openly.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

The state builds shared infrastructure; researchers and enterprises contribute to and learn from it.

Accountable

Restrict support to firms with an existing deployment. Fund implementation specialists, workflow redesign, governance and benefits measurement rather than software licences, drawing on leading adopters’ expertise and business co-investment.

THE RECIPROCAL COMMITMENT

The state supports deeper adoption; participating businesses co-invest and leading firms contribute expertise.
